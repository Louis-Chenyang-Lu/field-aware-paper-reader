# Lens: Clinical research, epidemiology and evidence synthesis

**How this community reads.** Evidence-based medicine reads papers through **critical appraisal**: Is the design valid for the question? Was it carried out soundly (risk of bias)? What are the results, and how precise? Do they apply to my population? Checklists like CASP start with screening questions — if the basic design is invalid, the rest of the appraisal matters less. When the paper does not say what was done, the answer is "can't tell", and many "can't tell" answers are themselves a warning.

## First: identify the design
RCT · cohort · case-control · cross-sectional · diagnostic accuracy · prognostic/prediction model · systematic review / meta-analysis · economic evaluation · case series. The design decides which checklist applies and how strong a causal claim can be.

## Questions to answer (adapt to design)
- **PICO**: Population, Intervention/exposure, Comparator, Outcome(s). Is the primary outcome pre-specified and clinically meaningful (not only a surrogate)?
- **RCTs**: How was randomization done and concealed? Who was blinded? Were groups similar at baseline? Loss to follow-up, and was analysis intention-to-treat? Was the trial registered, and do reported outcomes match the registration?
- **Observational**: How were exposure and outcome measured? Which confounders were adjusted for, and which plausible ones were not? Selection bias, reverse causation, immortal time bias?
- **Diagnostic**: Reference standard, spectrum of patients, sensitivity/specificity with CIs.
- **Prediction models**: Development vs validation, overfitting, calibration and discrimination, external validation.
- **Systematic reviews / meta-analyses**: Search strategy and databases, inclusion criteria, risk-of-bias assessment of included studies, heterogeneity, publication bias, certainty of evidence.
- **Results**: effect size with confidence interval (absolute and relative), number needed to treat/harm where relevant — not only p-values.
- **Applicability**: Would the results apply to other patients, settings, health systems? Benefits vs harms vs costs?

## Red flags
- Outcome switching vs registration; many secondary outcomes with one significant.
- Relative risk reported without absolute risk.
- Large attrition, per-protocol analysis only.
- Subgroup findings presented as main findings.
- Causal language from observational data without a causal design.

## Reporting standards
CONSORT (trials), STROBE (observational), STARD (diagnostic), TRIPOD (prediction models), PRISMA (systematic reviews), CHEERS (economic evaluation); appraisal tools: CASP checklists, Cochrane RoB 2, ROBINS-I, GRADE.
