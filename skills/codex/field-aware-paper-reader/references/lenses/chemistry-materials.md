# Lens: Chemistry and materials science

**How this community reads.** Chemists and materials scientists check whether the claimed compound, structure or property is really established: synthesis that can be reproduced, characterization that supports the identity and purity of what was made, and performance that is benchmarked fairly. The Supporting Information is often the largest part of the evidence (spectra, procedures, extra samples), so read it for the key claims. A claim resting on a single characterization technique is weaker than one confirmed by several independent techniques.

## Reading order
1. Abstract and TOC graphic: what was made or discovered, and the headline property or performance.
2. Main figures: structures, characterization (e.g. diffraction, spectroscopy, microscopy, thermal analysis), performance data.
3. Experimental section and SI: procedures, conditions, yields, characterization data for new compounds/materials.
4. Comparison with literature benchmarks.

## For each key figure
- Which technique, what it measures, how to read it (peaks, phases, morphology, curves), what conclusion is drawn.
- Does the data actually support that conclusion, or could another phase/impurity/artifact explain it?

## Questions to answer
- Is the identity and purity of the product established by several techniques? Are controls and blank experiments included?
- Is the synthesis reproducible: full conditions, scale, yields, number of batches?
- Performance claims: measured under standard conditions? Compared with the best existing materials under the same conditions and metrics? Stability/cycling/long-term data?
- Mechanism claims: supported by direct evidence (in situ measurements, isotope labelling, computation) or inferred?
- For computational chemistry: level of theory, basis set/functional, validation against experiment.

## Red flags
- One technique carrying the whole structural claim.
- Benchmarks against weak or non-comparable literature values.
- Only the best sample reported; no statistics across batches.
- Long-term stability or scale-up not tested when the application needs it.

## Write in the "Field lens" section
What was made/found → characterization evidence per claim (technique | what it shows | sufficient?) → performance vs fair benchmarks → mechanism evidence → reproducibility.
