# Lens: Qualitative and mixed-methods research

**How this community reads.** Qualitative research is judged on credibility, transparency and reflexivity, not on sample size or p-values. Readers ask who did the research and how their position could shape it, how participants were chosen, how data were collected and analysed, and whether the themes are grounded in the data. Reporting checklists such as COREQ (for interviews and focus groups) help readers gauge validity, but they have been criticized for encouraging box-ticking — use them as prompts, not as a score.

## Reading order
1. Research question and the methodological tradition (e.g. grounded theory, phenomenology, ethnography, thematic analysis, case study, discourse analysis).
2. Research team and reflexivity: who collected and analysed the data, their relationship to participants and to the topic.
3. Sampling and recruitment: who was included and why, who was missing, how the sample size was decided (saturation or other justification).
4. Data collection: setting, interview guide, recording, duration, field notes.
5. Analysis: coding process, number of coders, how themes were derived, how disagreements were handled, software.
6. Findings: themes and the quotations supporting them.
7. For mixed methods: how the qualitative and quantitative parts are integrated.

## Questions to answer
- Does the methodology fit the question?
- Are the themes supported by the data shown (quotes from several participants, not one), and are contradictory cases discussed?
- Is the researchers' influence acknowledged and handled?
- How transferable are the findings, and to which settings? (Qualitative work does not claim statistical generalization; check that it doesn't.)
- What would the participants or a different analyst plausibly have said differently?

## Red flags
- Methodology named but not actually followed.
- Themes that restate the interview questions.
- Quotes from a few vocal participants carrying the whole analysis.
- Quantified language ("most participants") without counts, or claims of generalization.

## Reporting standards
COREQ (interviews/focus groups), SRQR, CASP qualitative checklist; MMAT for mixed methods.

## Write in the "Field lens" section
Methodology and fit → reflexivity → sampling → analysis process → themes with how well each is supported → transferability.
